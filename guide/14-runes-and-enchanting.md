# Chapter 14 — Runes & Enchanting

The enchanting table on the Ridge speaks **runes**: custom enchants on top of the vanilla ones.

### Reading a Rune

Hover any enchanted item and the lore explains what each rune does.
`/enchants` (or `/runes`) opens the full guide: every rune, its tier, and what it conflicts with.
Tiers run Common → Uncommon → Rare → Legendary. Relics never come from a table roll.
A rune's name in your lore is the name in the guide, so look before you fight.
On an item with many runes the lore shortens to fit your screen: first the details go, then it lists the names only.
Hold the item and run `/enchants held` to read every rune on it in full.
Villagers' trade offers show the same rune lore, so you know what you're buying.

- **Rune Guide:** `/enchants`
- **Runes on This Item:** `/enchants held`

### Rolling Together

Every table roll gives you one rune for sure.
Your civic rank sets the chance of a **second** rune: Seedling 25%, Scout 35%, Ranger 45%, Pathfinder 55%, Scout Master 60%.
Enchant with another player standing within ten blocks and the chance rises another 10%.
Nothing pushes the odds past 70%. Enchanting is better done at camp than alone.
Rank buys you more runes, not stronger ones: a Seedling's Timber is the same Timber as a Scout Master's.
A plain book works at the table too: it can roll any rune, not just ones for a tool. Keep it, sell it, or put it on your gear with an anvil.

### Runes in the Wild

Villagers sometimes offer runed gear; so does loot in the world.
**Excavator** digs a cube at once, and every extra block wears the tool like a normal swing.
**Timber** is a Legendary axe rune from the table or world loot. One chop fells the whole tree, a full redwood included.
Every log it fells wears the axe like a normal chop, so bring Unbreaking. Sneak to take a single log and leave your cabin standing.
Runes you don't want can't be scrubbed at a grindstone the way vanilla enchants can. Trade the item instead.

### Ruin Runes

A second set of runes comes from the camp's dungeon structures and their librarians, not the enchanting table.
A librarian in the right ruin sometimes has a secret trade for one of these; the same ruin's loot chests can roll them too.

| Rune | What it does | Find it at |
|---|---|---|
| Aerial's Bane | Extra melee damage against anything flying. | The Lone Citadel, snowy ruins |
| Antidote | Shrugs off poison and wither damage; both burn off you faster. | Toxic Lairs |
| Curse of Conductivity | A curse: can call lightning down on you in a storm. | A witch hut's librarian |
| Ghasted | Your crossbow fires an actual fireball instead of a bolt. | Sealing Halls |
| Gravity | Hits pull the target in instead of knocking it back. | The End |
| Hydro Veil | On a mount harness: no fire buildup, no explosion knockback, slow healing. | Nether keeps, Sealing Halls |
| Illagers Bane | Extra melee damage against illagers. | Illager ruins and outposts |
| Tri-Shot | Your bow fires three arrows at once. | Toxic Lairs |
| Outreach | Reach a little further to interact with blocks and things. | A desert librarian's secret trade |
| Photosynthesis | Repairs itself slowly while you're outdoors in daylight. | A jungle librarian's secret trade |
| Blowthrough | Arrows punch through an extra enemy. | Toxic Lairs |
| Might | Crossbow bolts hit harder. | The Lone Citadel, snowy ruins |
| Spiteful | Extra damage on hit, but it gives your target a burst of Strength too. | Pale Residences, Ominous Shrines |
| Swift Soar | Sprint while riding a harnessed mount to fly faster. | Nether keeps, ports, Sealing Halls |
| Traveler | Step up slopes without jumping, walk a little faster. | Shrines |
| Wax Wings | Your elytra takes less damage flying fast, almost none up high. | The End |
| Wither Coated | Sword hits apply Wither; short and strong at high levels. | Nether keeps, Sealing Halls |

These haven't been sorted into the Common/Uncommon/Rare/Legendary tiers above yet, so `/enchants` doesn't list them alongside the table runes.
Hover a runed item to read what it does, or check this table.

### Armor Trims

A trim on your armor does more than look good: each trimmed piece gives a bonus while you wear it.
The bonus comes from the trim's material, and every trimmed piece adds its own, so a full set of four gives four times as much.
Hover a trimmed piece and its lore says what the trim does.

| Trim material | Each piece gives |
|---|---|
| Emerald | Hero of the Village +1 level: cheaper villager trades. A full set is Hero IV. |
| Lapis | +25% XP from orbs. A full set doubles your XP. |
| Iron | +7.5% attack speed and +5% attack damage |
| Diamond | +1.5 armor and +0.5 armor toughness |
| Gold | +10% mining speed |
| Redstone | +5% walking speed and +15% sneaking speed |
| Quartz | -15% fall damage and 1 more block before a fall hurts. A full set roughly halves fall damage. |
| Netherite | 10% less time on fire |
| Resin | +15% knockback resistance |
| Copper | +0.75 blocks of reach |
| Amethyst | +0.5 Luck: better fishing and loot rolls |

Only these eleven materials do anything. The other trim colors, glowing ones included, are for looks.
Lapis counts for whoever is closest when the XP appears, so a friend in lapis armor right next to you shares the bonus.
